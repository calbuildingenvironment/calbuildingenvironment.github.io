Lead Inspection Services

Independent Lead-Based Paint Evaluations

Lead-based paint may be present in older residential, commercial, institutional, and industrial buildings. Our inspections help identify painted surfaces that may require further evaluation before renovation, demolition, maintenance, or property transactions.

When laboratory confirmation is appropriate, representative paint chip samples are collected using HUD/EPA protocols (XRF screening with confirmatory paint chip sampling) and submitted to AIHA-LAP, LLC accredited laboratories for AAS or ICP analysis per EPA SW-846 7000B/3050B. Laboratory findings are incorporated into a detailed inspection report with component-by-component results, lead concentrations (mg/cm² or % by weight), and hazard classifications per HUD/EPA standards.

Because California Building Environment does not perform lead paint removal or remediation, our inspections remain independent and objective.

When Is an Inspection Recommended?

Common Reasons for Lead-Based Paint Inspections

Lead-based paint inspections are required for pre-1978 target housing under EPA RRP Rule and HUD Lead Safe Housing Rule, prior to renovation/demolition of commercial/industrial structures, for property transactions involving pre-1978 buildings, and to support OSHA Lead in Construction Standard compliance.

Pre-Renovation / EPA RRP Rule

The EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule requires certified firms and trained renovators to test for lead-based paint or presume its presence in pre-1978 housing and child-occupied facilities before disturbing painted surfaces.

Pre-Demolition

Complete or partial demolition of pre-1978 structures requires lead-based paint identification to comply with OSHA Lead in Construction (29 CFR 1926.62), Cal/OSHA (8 CCR 1532.1), and hazardous waste disposal regulations.

Pre-1978 Residential Properties

Homes built before the 1978 federal ban on residential lead-based paint may contain lead coatings on windows, doors, trim, siding, porches, and interior surfaces. Inspections identify lead hazards per HUD Guidelines.

Commercial & Industrial Facilities

Industrial coatings, structural steel primers, and architectural paints in older commercial buildings may contain lead. Inspections support renovation planning and worker protection programs.

Property Transactions & Disclosure

Federal law requires disclosure of known lead-based paint and lead hazards in pre-1978 housing sales and leases. Inspections provide documentation for buyers, sellers, landlords, and property managers.

HUD & Federally Assisted Housing

HUD-assisted housing, Section 8, and public housing require lead-based paint risk assessments, clearance examinations, and ongoing reevaluations per 24 CFR Part 35.

Lead Risk Assessments

Beyond Paint: Dust Wipe & Soil Sampling

For multi-unit housing, public facilities, and property transactions, a lead risk assessment may be more appropriate than a paint-only inspection. A risk assessment identifies lead hazards in paint, dust, and soil, and results support corrective action planning and regulatory compliance.

What a Risk Assessment Covers

Identifying Lead Hazards in Paint, Dust & Soil

A lead risk assessment goes beyond painted surfaces to evaluate potential exposure pathways across the property. Common elements include:

  • Paint chip sampling on painted surfaces
  • Dust wipe sampling on floors, windowsills, and window wells
  • Soil sampling in play areas and near building foundations
  • Identification of deteriorated paint and friction surfaces
  • Documentation of findings for corrective action planning
Who Typically Requests One

Public Housing, Multi-Unit & Transaction Projects

Risk assessments are commonly requested for HUD-regulated public and federally assisted housing, multi-unit rental properties, and pre-1978 properties changing hands. Reports include documented findings and corrective action recommendations that support regulatory requirements such as the HUD Lead Safe Housing Rule and Cal/OSHA lead standards. All dust wipe and soil samples are analyzed by NVLAP-accredited laboratories.

Frequently Asked Questions

Lead-Based Paint Inspection FAQs

Do you perform laboratory lead testing?

No. We collect representative paint samples during the inspection when appropriate. Those samples are submitted to accredited laboratories for analysis. Laboratory analysis is performed by the accredited laboratory.

Do you remove lead-based paint?

No. California Building Environment provides independent inspections and consulting only. We do not perform lead paint abatement or remediation services.

Will every inspection require paint samples?

Not necessarily. The inspection scope depends on the project, the building, and the client's objectives. Representative samples are collected only when appropriate.

How long does a lead-based paint inspection take?

Inspection times vary depending on the size of the property, accessibility, and the number of painted surfaces requiring evaluation. Most residential inspections take 2-4 hours on site; larger projects are scoped to the property.

When will I receive the laboratory results?

Turnaround time depends on the accredited laboratory and the selected analysis schedule. Results are included with your final inspection report.

Regulatory Framework

Lead-based paint inspections in California are governed by federal and state regulations designed to protect occupants from lead exposure:

  • EPA RRP Rule — The Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule requires lead-safe work practices and inspections for pre-1978 buildings.
  • CDPH Lead Compliance Program — California Department of Public Health administers the lead-safe housing and lead compliance programs.
  • Cal/OSHA Lead Standards — California Occupational Safety and Health Administration's lead-in-construction standard (Title 8, Section 1532.1) requires exposure assessments.
  • HUD Lead Safe Housing Rule — Federal lead safety requirements for federally assisted housing.

Our inspectors are California CDPH-certified lead inspectors and risk assessors. All dust wipe samples are analyzed by NVLAP-accredited laboratories.
